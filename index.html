Why Ghana, why now

Ghana is West Africa's most accessible entry point — stable, English-speaking, and welcoming, with a growing "Year of Return" pilgrimage drawing the diaspora home.

In one trip: 500-year-old coastal fortresses, rainforest canopy walks, savanna elephants, kente-weaving villages, and some of the region's best-kept beaches. This lookbook moves coast to savanna — Cape Coast, Accra, Kumasi, Mole — as a route you can actually drive.

CENTRAL REGION

Cape Coast & Elmina Castles

UNESCO World HeritageTwo of the largest of over 40 fortifications along Ghana's coast, built by European traders from the 15th century on.
The Door of No ReturnA profoundly moving stop for understanding the trans-Atlantic slave trade and the diaspora's roots.
Pair it withA walk through Cape Coast's fishing harbor at dawn, when the wooden boats come in.
CENTRAL REGION

Kakum National Park Canopy Walkway

Seven rope bridgesSuspended up to 40 meters above the rainforest floor — Ghana's most photographed adventure.
WildlifeHome to forest elephants, over 300 bird species, and Diana monkeys (best spotted at sunrise).
Good to knowArrive early — the walkway gets busy and hot by mid-morning.

GREATER ACCRA

Accra: Jamestown to Osu

JamestownColorful colonial-era fishing quarter, boxing gyms, and a lighthouse with sweeping coastal views.
Independence Square & Kwame Nkrumah MausoleumThe monumental heart of Ghana's 1957 independence — first in sub-Saharan Africa.
Osu & Accra's art sceneGalleries, live highlife music, and the city's best chop bars and rooftop bars.
ASHANTI REGION

Kumasi & the Ashanti Heartland

Manhyia PalaceHome of the Asantehene, spiritual and cultural seat of the Ashanti Kingdom.
BonwireThe kente-weaving village where the iconic strip-woven cloth originated — watch looms in motion.
Kejetia MarketOne of West Africa's largest markets — a sensory deep-dive into daily Ghanaian life.

NORTHERN REGION

Mole National Park

Ghana's largest wildlife refugeWalking safaris that bring you close to elephants, antelope, and baboons — rare on foot in Africa.
Larabanga MosqueA striking Sudanese-style mud-and-timber mosque, among West Africa's oldest.
Getting thereA long but worthwhile drive north — best combined with an overnight at the park motel.
COASTLINE

Beaches: Busua, Anomabo & Ada

BusuaGhana's laid-back surf town — board rentals, beach bars, and reliable swells.
AnomaboBoutique beach lodges beside a working fishing village, close to Cape Coast.
Ada FoahWhere the Volta River meets the Atlantic — kayaking, river islands, and sandbar sunsets.

CULTURE

Festivals & Living Traditions

Homowo & PanafestHarvest festivals and the biennial Pan-African Historical Theatre Festival draw the diaspora each year.
Adinkra symbolsVisual proverbs — stamped onto cloth — that encode Akan philosophy in a single mark.
Drumming & danceAdowa, kpanlogo, and talking drums remain part of everyday celebration, not performance for tourists.

Jollof riceThe dish behind West Africa's friendliest culinary rivalry — try Ghana's smoky version and judge yourself.
Banku & grilled tilapiaFermented corn-and-cassava dough, best eaten by hand with a fiery pepper sauce.
Kelewele & red redSpiced fried plantain, and a black-eyed-pea stew fried in palm oil — street food royalty.

Plan your trip

Best time to visit

November–March: dry season, cooler nights, easiest travel between regions.

Getting around

Domestic flights Accra–Kumasi–Tamale save time; road trips reveal the most.

Entry

Most visitors need a visa or e-visa in advance — apply at least a few weeks ahead.

Suggested route

Accra → Cape Coast → Kakum → Kumasi → Mole, 10–14 days end to end.
